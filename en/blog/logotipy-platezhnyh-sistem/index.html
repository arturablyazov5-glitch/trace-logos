Payment System Logos — Mir, Visa, Mastercard, SBP

A payment system logo is the most mass‑reproduced mark of any kind: printed on billions of cards, checkout stickers and pay buttons in every online store. Mistakes here cost the most, so the category evolves slowly and carefully. Let's decode the marks of the main payment systems and services — and explain why they look more austere than everyone else.

TL;DR

A payment mark's job is instant trust at checkout‑sticker size. Hence the extreme simplicity: the two Mastercard circles, the Visa word, the Mir globe, the SBP lightning bolt. All marks are in the Payments & Cards category.

Mastercard: two circles that need no name

Mastercard is the benchmark of simplification. The red and yellow interlocking circles date to 1968; 2016 cleaned the mark (the overlap went flat), and in 2019 the company took the radical step of dropping the word entirely. The circles work unsigned: research showed over 80% of people recognize the mark without the name. That's branding's top league — the "nameless" club contains Apple, Nike and few others — exactly the brands we cover in why brands simplify their logos. The overlap stands for partnership — the two sides of a transaction connecting.

Visa: a word in blue

Visa has always been a wordmark: four letters, italic, blue. The yellow stroke (a legacy of the first cards' two‑color flag) came and went — today the mark is blue with gradient accents. Visa's bet is the opposite of Mastercard's: not a symbol but the name itself, repeated billions of times — a strategy that works with a short memorable word — see how the name shapes the mark for more.

Mir: a globe with a southern hemisphere

Russia's Mir system (2015) chose a name with a double meaning — "the world" and "peace" — and a globe mark with green‑blue arcs. The logo follows category canon: simple forms, calm colors, readability at card‑chip size. The telling detail: the arcs form a stylized hemisphere echoing the name's global ambition.

SBP: the fast‑payment lightning

SBP — the Faster Payments System — is the youngest mark here and the only one with an "energetic" metaphor: a multicolor zigzag bolt. The logic is direct: the system's core promise is speed, and lightning is its universal symbol. The multicolor reflects the interbank nature: SBP belongs to no single bank but connects them all — the same logic behind how Russian bank marks work.

Wallets and pay services

Above the card networks lives a layer of payment services, their marks built to one template — "platform name + Pay":

  • Apple Pay — the apple + the word Pay: all the branding is carried by the apple.
  • Yandex Pay — Yandex's signature badge.
  • YooMoney and YooKassa — the violet "Ю": a rare Cyrillic monogram in this role.
  • PayPal — two italic "P"s: the online‑payment veteran with the most fintech‑y blue.
  • Stripe — just the word: an infrastructure brand that doesn't need to charm end buyers.

The vanishing medium: the card moves into the phone

A quiet revolution of media awaits the category: the physical card — the payment mark's main stage for half a century — is yielding to tokenized phone payments and QR checkout. That changes what the marks must do. On a POS terminal screen and inside a banking app the logo shows for seconds at an even smaller size than on a card. And a new medium appeared — the payment confirmation animation: the Apple Pay checkmark, the SBP wave. Marks born for embossed plastic are learning to animate — a rare case of the most conservative category being forced to change faster than the fashionable ones.

Why they're all so austere

ConstraintConsequence for the mark
Card printing (micro size, embossing)simple forms, mandatory monochrome
A checkout sticker among other systemsdiffer by color, not detail
A pay button on anyone's sitethe mark must not "argue" with foreign design
Trust in moneyno experiments, no trends (why)

A payment mark is effectively a road sign: it expresses no individuality, it transmits the standardized signal "you can pay here". That's why the category preserves its forms for decades and changes only the polish.

A lesson for online stores

When placing payment logos on your site, use official files without recoloring or distortion — payment systems enforce the rules on using others' logos more strictly than anyone. Correct SVGs of all systems are in our catalog.
