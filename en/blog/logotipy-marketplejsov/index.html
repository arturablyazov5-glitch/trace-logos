Marketplace Logos — Ozon, Wildberries, Avito and More

Marketplaces are the only category whose logo tens of millions of people see daily in four roles at once: app icon, site header, the box at the door and the pickup‑point sign. Let's unpack the marks of the market's main players, why wordmarks won here, and how the brands divide up colors.

TL;DR

Nearly all marketplaces use wordmarks: the name matters more than a symbol when the brand is "the place you go for everything". Differentiation rests on color: blue — Ozon, violet‑magenta gradient — Wildberries, black — Lamoda, red‑orange — AliExpress. All marks are in the Marketplaces & Retail category.

Why marketplaces use wordmarks

In a category with infinite assortment, no symbol fits: any picture (a cart, a box, a bag) narrows the image to one association. So the market almost unanimously chose the wordmark — the name in a custom face (the type is covered in types of logos). The "text doesn't fit the icon" problem gets solved individually: Ozon wins with four letters, Wildberries shortens to "WB", Yandex Market to an "M" in the signature badge.

Ozon: a short name as a superpower

Ozon is a Runet veteran (since 1998, started with books like Amazon) and owner of a rare asset: a four‑letter name that fits into an app icon whole. Its blue is so established that every ecosystem service inherits it — Ozon Bank is recognized instantly. The short name is exactly what spared Ozon a separate monogram — one form works everywhere — we cover logo sizes for platforms separately.

Wildberries: a gradient against the field

Wildberries long lived with an austere wordmark, then in the 2020s switched to a violet‑magenta gradient — the category's boldest color move. The logic is visible: blue (Ozon), red (AliExpress) and yellow (Yandex's marketplace arm) were taken, and a gradient claims a whole zone of spectrum at once. The long name still demands a short version — "WB" lives in the icon and in the subsidiary Wildberries Bank. On the gradient fashion: logo trends 2026.

Avito: multicolor dots

Avito is a classifieds board rather than a classic marketplace, and its mark solves a different problem: showing variety. The signature device is a cluster of colored circles next to the name: each dot reads as a "category" (cars, housing, goods, jobs). A rare combination mark with abstract graphics in this category — and one of the Runet's most recognized brands overall.

AliExpress: parent‑brand heritage

AliExpress wears red‑orange and a wordmark with a "smile" flourish — the DNA of parent Alibaba. Warm colors in e‑commerce drive impulse: red and orange traditionally connect to discounts and urgency — we cover color psychology in logo design separately — the opposite strategy to Ozon's calm blue.

Niche players: black as positioning

  • Lamoda — a strict black wordmark: fashion sells through restraint; a "fashionable" logo would age in a season.
  • Golden Apple — a laconic apple emblem: a beauty retailer can afford a symbol because its category is narrower than "everything".
  • Ecosystem marketplaces inherit their parents' identity — like Yandex Market living in the yellow Yandex badge.

The market color map

BlueOzonreliability, "all is well"

As in banking and messengers, color is territory: overlaps are rare, and a newcomer must hunt for free spectrum.

The cardboard test

Marketplaces own a medium nobody else has: the cardboard box and the bag. The mark must survive single‑color print on brown board — the harshest simplicity test there is. Open the marketplace category and mentally print each mark in one color: they all survive, and that's no coincidence — we explain why monochrome versions exist separately.

Takeaways

  1. The name is the main asset. When you sell everything, a symbol is powerless: the name, the color and repetition do the work.
  2. A short name saves you a whole logo version. Ozon lives as one form; long names need monograms.
  3. Color is divided like radio frequencies. Claim a zone — then defend it for years and paint everything in it: boxes, pickup points, bank cards.
  4. Ecosystems show in the mark. Subsidiary services inherit the identity — you can guess the parent marketplace from its bank's logo.
