A Logo for Your Telegram Channel — An Avatar That Stands Out in the Feed

In the Telegram feed your avatar is a circle less than a centimeter across. It's how subscribers find your channel among a hundred chats, and how a passing visitor decides whether the project looks serious. Yet avatars are usually thrown together in five minutes — with the same five mistakes every time. Here's how to make a circle that works: visible, legible, respectable.

TL;DR

The uploaded file is a square, 512×512 minimum (PNG or JPG), always displayed as a circle. The working formula: one large element (mark, letter, face) on a contrasting solid background, no small text, tested at 40 pixels. A horizontal logo with the name is the worst option: the circular crop leaves an unreadable stump.

The technical requirements

  • Size: Telegram wants a square of at least 512×512; upload 1024×1024 for retina headroom (why the 2× margin matters: the blurry logo article).
  • Format: PNG or JPG. No transparency — transparent areas flood white, so design your own background.
  • Crop: only the inscribed circle shows. The square's corners are always cut — nothing important goes there.
  • Where it lives: the chat list (~50 px), the channel header, reposts, comments (~40 px), search. Design for the smallest size, not the pretty big one.

The formula

1. One element, large

In a fingernail‑sized circle only one hero survives: a mark, a single letter, a simple symbol or a face. It should fill 60–80% of the circle. A timid little logo in an empty field becomes a dot in the feed.

2. A contrasting solid background

Your avatar sits among dozens of others on the interface's light and dark backgrounds. Clean saturated fills with a white or black mark work best: yellow, red, blue, green. Check the avatars of channels in your topic — then pick a different color: in a feed, difference wins (the color psychology logic).

3. No text — or one‑two letters

The channel name is printed right next to the avatar — duplicating it inside the circle is pointless, and small text at 40 pixels is physically unreadable. If you want letters — a one‑two letter monogram: "BC" works, "Business Club Online 24/7" doesn't.

4. The 40-pixel test

The main check: shrink the design to 40×40 and judge honestly — is the element distinct, the letter readable, the background alive? Also check dark mode: a navy background, striking on white, dissolves in the dark theme.

The five classic mistakes
  1. A horizontal logo with the name — the circular crop leaves a stripe of chopped letters.
  2. A detailed photo — a screenshot, a collage, a group shot: noise at 40 px.
  3. Small text — slogans, addresses, "subscribe": unreadable.
  4. A plain white background — merges with the light theme into an empty circle.
  5. Content in the square's corners — the circle cuts them off.

A mark for a channel, a face for a person

  • Personal brand: a close‑up face, solid background, good light. A face catches the eye better than any graphic — that's biology, not design.
  • Project/business: the mark or monogram on the brand color. If a logo exists — use its mark (not the full logo!) per the rules in logo sizes for social media.
  • Topic channel: a simple symbol of the theme — a bolt, a book, a chart. An emoji works well: download a high‑res PNG from our emoji catalog and set it on a colored background — fast and neat.

Making one for free in 20 minutes

  1. Figma (free): a 1024×1024 square, fill it with color, center the mark/letter/emoji with 10–20% margins.
  2. For a monogram use a bold geometric face (Inter Bold, Manrope ExtraBold — free, license‑clean: see fonts and licensing).
  3. Apply a circular mask to preview the real crop — but export the unmasked square.
  4. Test at 40 px in both themes.
  5. Export PNG → upload in channel settings.

A base for the avatar can come from our materials too: grab an SVG mark from the logo catalog, recolor it right on the page — and you have your foundation.

The avatar is part of a system

Don't forget the neighboring carriers: the same concept should live in your other social avatars, the site favicon and the covers. One mark, one color, one logic — that's how subscribers recognize you on any platform. How this small‑carrier system is built: the app icon article.

In short

A 1024×1024 square → one large element → a contrasting solid background → nothing important in the corners → a 40-pixel test in both themes. Horizontal logos and small text — out. Twenty minutes in Figma and your circle stops drowning in the feed.

Building blocks — vector marks and high‑res emoji — are in our logo and emoji catalogs: download, recolor, set on a background — done.
