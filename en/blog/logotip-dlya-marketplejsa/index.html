A Logo for a Marketplace — How to Stand Out in the Ozon and Wildberries Feed

A shopper on a marketplace has about half a second for your card. They scroll the feed with a thumb, dozens of products fly past, and the eye catches only what's instantly clear and different from its neighbors. In this thumbnail race a brand logo isn't "for respectability" but a working tool: it helps the shopper recognize you among a hundred identical cards and come back to you specifically. Let's break down how to prepare a logo and brand so they work on Ozon, Wildberries, and other platforms instead of getting lost.

The main battlefield is the thumbnail

All the shopper sees in the feed is a tiny card image the size of a fingernail. And here the same rule applies as for a favicon: a logo readable on a big screen can turn into a murky blob as a thumbnail.

Hence the first requirement — the mark on the card and packaging must be simple and large. Thin lines, small text, and complex details will vanish in the feed. If your logo is complex, use its simplified version — exactly the principle from an adaptive logo: a dedicated version for small size.

A white background is both friend and trap

Marketplaces usually require a white or light card background. For a logo this means two things.

First, the mark must not "stick" to the white and dissolve into it: a too‑light logo on a white background is barely visible. Sufficient contrast is needed — part of a mark's accessibility.

Second, if you overlay the logo on a product photo or colored packaging, it needs a transparent background, not a white rectangle that gives away a "sticker" And since cards and previews are always raster, keep a quality PNG exported from a vector source handy so it doesn't "blur" Why the source must be vector — in a separate article.

Recognizability beats card "beauty"

On a marketplace a logo solves a business task, not an aesthetic one: so that a shopper who liked your product finds your brand next time among similar ones. So the mark must sit on cards the same — in one corner, one size, one look. A mismatch breaks recognizability just as in brand consistency.

A logo lives not only in the card

A brand on a marketplace isn't one picture. The mark appears in several places at once, and each dictates its own requirements:

  • Store avatar — often cropped to a circle or square; prepare a compact version, as for a round avatar.
  • Store cover/banner — here the full version of the mark and brand colors work.
  • Packaging and inserts — this is already print: vector, a single‑color version, and a color model matter.
  • Card infographics — the logo as a caption, unobtrusive but recognizable.

For all this to look like one brand, the mark is worth designing as a set of versions for different places — essentially a mini design system.

A quick checklist before launching on a platform

  • Is the logo readable at card thumbnail size?
  • Is there enough contrast with a light background?
  • Is a PNG with a transparent background ready for overlays?
  • Is there a compact version for the store avatar?
  • For packaging — are there vector and single‑color versions?
  • Does the mark sit the same on all cards?

If you want to dig deeper into what marks exist and what makes them strong, start with the foundational article what a logo is. And to see how the platforms' own logos are built, check the overview marketplace logos.

In short

On a marketplace a logo has a business task — to help a shopper recognize and remember the brand among a hundred similar cards, not "add respectability" The main battlefield is a fingernail‑sized thumbnail, so the mark must be simple, large, and readable at small size, as in the adaptive approach. The platforms' white background requires sufficient contrast and a transparent PNG for overlays, and packaging requires vector and single‑color versions. And most importantly — the mark must sit the same everywhere: recognizability on a marketplace is built on consistency, not variety.
