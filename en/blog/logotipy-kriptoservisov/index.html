Crypto Service Logos — Binance, Coinbase, MetaMask and More

The crypto industry started with a single visual cliché — a gold coin with a letter — and in ten years covered a road that took banks a century: from garage aesthetics to strict fintech minimalism. Today crypto marks are more interesting than bank ones: the industry must look trustworthy (money!) and rebellious (decentralization!) at the same time. Here is how Binance, Coinbase, MetaMask, Crypto.com and Exodus resolve that conflict.

TL;DR

The category stretches between two poles: fintech rigor — the blue Coinbase circle, the Crypto.com geometry — and crypto idiosyncrasy — the yellow diamond maze of Binance, the 3D fox of MetaMask. The closer a service sits to mainstream users and regulators, the calmer the mark; the deeper into web3, the stranger and brighter. Files are in the Payments & Cards category.

Coinbase: a bank that happens to do crypto

Coinbase bet on the opposite of crypto chaos: a calm blue wordmark and a round "C"-coin icon. No lightning bolts, no diamonds, no cyberpunk — the palette and shapes come from classic fintech, and the blue is the same trust code as banks and payment systems — we cover color psychology separately. The logic is transparent: the first exchange to go public in the US and work with regulators must look like a financial institution, not an enthusiast forum.

Binance: a diamond as a private alphabet

Binance went the other way: a golden‑yellow mark of diamonds arranged into a maze‑like "B". The shape nods to two layers at once — the gold of coins (the old cliché, abstracted) and the pixel‑block aesthetics of the blockchain. Yellow on black is the category's highest‑contrast pair and effectively the exchange's whole brand code, down to its BNB token inheriting the mark's shape. A rare case of a brand growing an entire alphabet out of its logo: the Binance diamond works as a monogram, a pattern and an icon at once.

MetaMask: the fox versus the suits

MetaMask — a polygonal 3D fox, the most unconventional mark in finance, period. The web3 wallet deliberately looks like a game character rather than a vault: its audience treats decentralization as ideology, and corporate minimalism would read as betrayal. The fox is a full mascot: it is animated, its eyes follow your cursor at login, and it became the badge of a whole generation of crypto apps. The parallel with the Firefox fox is no accident — both brands sell "the free internet", and both picked a fox.

Crypto.com and Exodus: the middle of the spectrum

Crypto.com — a blue‑and‑white heraldic lion shield: a claim to status and permanence, amplified by the brand's main asset — the domain‑as‑name. Stadium and Formula 1 sponsorships demand a mark that stands next to sports club crests — and it is engineered exactly for that.

Exodus — gradient geometry in purples: the language of neither bank nor game but of a "beautiful product" — the wallet always competed on interface design. The gradient is the same "new tech" code as in AI logos, one of the main logo trends overall.

The takeaway: a crypto mark is a statement on the industry's core conflict. The blue circle says "we are the new bank", the yellow diamond says "our territory, our rules", the fox says "we're not about banks at all". The choice of mark here is more honest than the press releases.

On usage

Exchange and wallet logos are registered trademarks, and finance is policed especially hard: another exchange's mark on your landing page easily reads as a partnership claim. Before placing one, read the rules on using others' logos.
