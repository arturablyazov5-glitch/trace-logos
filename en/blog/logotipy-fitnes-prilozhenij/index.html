Fitness App Logos — Strava, Nike Run Club, Peloton and More

The health category is a rare case where one app‑store shelf splits into two opposite visual languages. Sports services shout: sharp angles, motion, orange and red. Meditation services whisper: soft shapes, pastels, plenty of air. Here are the logos of the main fitness and wellness apps — and how the design reveals the product's promise before you even install it.

TL;DR

The category speaks two languages: dynamics — the Strava chevron, the Nike Run Club swoosh, the Peloton "P"-wheel — and calm — the gradient sky of Calm, the orange blob of Headspace. What they share: extremely simple shapes, because the mark lives on a tiny watch screen. Files are in the Health category.

The sports pole: angle, slant, motion

Strava — an orange mark of two chevrons fused into a stylized "M"-summit: at once a mountain, an upward arrow and a route trace on a map. Sharp angles and signature orange are pure achievement aggression; a brand whose social mechanics are built on competition looks like competition — we cover color psychology in logos separately.

Nike Run Club — a derivative brand: the Nike swoosh plus running context. The stroke drawn in 1971 for 35 dollars remains probably the purest expression of motion in graphic history — NRC simply inherits its energy. A classic "company mark + product marks" system, like the Yandex ecosystem.

Peloton — a red "P" whose counterform hides a bicycle wheel with a spoke: monogram and pictogram in one letter — we cover what a monogram is and logos with hidden meanings separately.

MapMyRun and AllTrails work with the landscape: a route line and a mountain silhouette. The trail, the summit and the GPS trace are the base hieroglyphs of the whole activity‑tracker segment.

The calm pole: circle, gradient, air

Calm — the word itself over a gradient sky: a mood logo where the meaning is carried by atmosphere, not shape. The app icon is a rare case of the background mattering more than the mark.

Headspace — an orange blob‑circle with a perfectly flat bottom: a meditating figure reduced to a spot. The friendly imperfection is deliberate: meditation here is a warm daily habit, not esoterics. The brand leans heavily on mascots — drawn characters in the same style.

Fitbit — a diamond of dots: pulse, steps, discrete data. A mark speaking data, not emotion — a legacy of the era when a fitness tracker was a geek gadget, not jewelry.

The utility row: platform marks

A separate group is the system health apps: Apple Health with its heart on white, Apple Fitness with the activity rings, Habitica with its pixel gamification aesthetic, habit trackers like Streaks. Apple's activity rings are an interesting case: a UI element (three progress rings) became a standalone mark recognized without a caption — the same logic that drives app icon design.

The pattern is immediate: sport takes the angle and the hot color, calm takes the circle and the gradient. Orange is the only color living on both poles: at Strava it means "energy", at Headspace — "warmth". Color sets the temperature; shape sets the meaning.

What to take away

Building an activity product — the mark needs a motion vector: a slant, an arrow, a diagonal. Building recovery and sleep — a circle, a horizontal, a soft gradient. Mixing the languages is dangerous: "aggressive meditation" and "calm interval running" are equally unsellable. And test the mark at watch‑screen size — in this category 40 pixels matter more than a billboard — we cover logo sizes separately.
