SVG vs PNG for Logos — Which to Choose

When you download a brand logo, you almost always face a choice between two formats — SVG and PNG. They look similar on screen, but work in fundamentally different ways. Let's figure out which one to use for each task.

SVG vs PNG: The Core Difference

SVG is a vector format. The logo is described mathematically — lines, curves, fills. This means it scales to any size without losing quality, from a 16×16 favicon to a billboard banner.

PNG is a raster format. The image is made of pixels. If you scale a PNG beyond its original size, you'll get jagged edges and blurriness.

When to Use SVG

  • Websites and interfaces. SVG has a smaller file size, scales to any screen, and stays crisp on Retina displays.
  • Figma, Sketch and other editors. Vector can be recolored, broken into parts, edited freely.
  • Print. Print shops require vector — it doesn't lose quality at any size.

When to Use PNG

  • Email and messengers. Not all email clients render SVG correctly — PNG is more reliable.
  • Presentations. PowerPoint and Google Slides work better with PNG.
  • Social media. Uploading profile pictures and cover images usually requires a raster format.
  • Complex logos with effects — gradients, shadows, textures that are difficult to reproduce in vector.

How to Convert SVG to PNG

Every logo page in our catalog has both buttons — download SVG and PNG. If you need a PNG at a custom size, open the SVG in Figma or a browser and export at the resolution you need.

The Short Answer

When in doubt — take SVG. It's the "source file" from which you can always get a PNG at any size. PNG is for places where SVG isn't supported: email, presentations, some social networks.
