AI Company Logos — How Neural Network Services Brand Themselves

In three years AI services have formed a visual language of their own — so cohesive that an "AI logo" is recognizable at a glance: a gradient, a glow, a sparkle or a kaleidoscopic rosette. Let's examine the marks of the main players — from ChatGPT to GigaChat — extract the shared devices and see who managed not to dissolve into the house style.

TL;DR

AI branding has three signature devices: the sparkle (a "magic" symbol inherited from the ✨ emoji), gradient blends (spectrum = the variety of possible answers) and organic knot‑rosettes (a nod to neural connections). The industry's problem is total sameness. The standouts went against the current: Midjourney's handwritten "M", Claude's minimalist serifs. All marks are in the AI category of our catalog.

The shared language: three devices of AI identity

1. The sparkle ✨

The four‑pointed star became the de facto "AI here" pictogram across interfaces — from "improve my text" buttons to "AI‑generated" badges. Its root is the ✨ emoji, long used to mark "magic" (on emoji language: popular emoji meanings). Gemini made the sparkle its actual logo, and dozens of services embedded it into their marks as a universal marker.

2. Gradient and glow

We covered the trend in logo trends 2026: smooth multicolor transitions became the visual synonym of generativity — the spectrum as a metaphor for the infinite answer space. Gemini shimmers blue‑violet, GigaChat wears the Sber ecosystem gradient, and Copilot sports a whole rainbow in its loop badge.

3. The knot‑rosette

An organic figure of interwoven petals or threads — a hint at a neural network. The canonical example is the ChatGPT "flower": a six‑fold knot of interlaced arcs that became as much a symbol of its era as the Twitter bird once was (more in the history of the ChatGPT logo). DeepSeek (a whale of wave segments) and a raft of second‑wave startups use the same knot logic.

The main marks, decoded

  • ChatGPT — a monochrome rosette. Telling: the world's most popular AI product does without gradients or color at all — a black‑and‑white mark banks recognition where everyone else glitters. The classic leader's move: when the category dazzles, restraint stands out.
  • Claude — an orange‑terracotta "burst" rosette and a serif wordmark. Warm color and bookish typography deliberately humanize the brand against competitors' cold techno‑gloss.
  • Gemini — the four‑pointed sparkle in a blue‑violet gradient. The mark set the fashion so thoroughly that its shape became the industry‑wide AI pictogram — a rare case of a product logo turning into a category icon. In 2025 the Gemini gradient even bled into Google's G.
  • Midjourney — a sailboat-"M" in a light handwritten stroke. No gradient, no sparkle: the artistic service chose an artistic mark and won recognition.
  • GigaChat — inherits Sber's gradient DNA: ecosystem colors on a shape hinting at dialogue. An example of fitting an AI product into an umbrella brand (how such systems work: what is a visual identity).
  • Alice — a circle with a "pulse": born from the voice assistant's audio wave, still one of the Russian internet's most recognizable AI brands.
  • Perplexity — a geometric "book‑portal" of intersecting planes: the knowledge search engine chose a metaphor of structure, not magic.
  • Grok — a hard monochrome diagonal in the spirit of X: a defiantly anti‑cute mark extending the rebel positioning.

Why they all look alike — and why that's a risk

The monotony has a simple cause: the category is new, everyone is explaining the same thing ("our AI is smart"), and everyone watches the pioneers. The result is what designers call a "sea of sameness": gradient + sparkle + rounded sans. To users it's already indistinguishable — meaning the device stopped working; we wrote about this effect separately.

A lesson for your own projects

If you're building an AI product, the default "gradient + sparkle" kit today says not "we're innovative" but "we're like everyone". The category's strong brands — ChatGPT, Claude, Midjourney — stand out precisely by dropping part of the cliché. How to find your own move: types of logos and color psychology.
