How to Prepare a Site for AI — GEO and llms.txt in 2026

More and more often a user doesn't open ten sites from search results but gets a ready answer from a neural network — with a couple of links to sources. This changes the rules of the game: now it matters not only to appear in search but to be "understandable" to an AI assistant. The approach that solves this is called GEO. Let's break down what it is, what the llms.txt file and structured data have to do with it, and how to make a site and brand visible in AI answers.

TL;DR

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is optimizing a site for neural networks, the analog of SEO for the era of AI search. For an assistant to "see" and correctly cite you, a site needs machine‑readable data: Schema markup, a clear structure, FAQ blocks, and an llms.txt file — a content "map" for AI. The role of the favicon grows too: it's shown next to the link in an AI answer.

What GEO is and how it differs from SEO

SEO (Search Engine Optimization) is optimizing for search engines: so a site ranks in Google and Yandex results. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the same, but for neural networks: so an AI assistant finds your content, understands it correctly, and cites it in its answer.

The difference is the consumer. A search engine shows a list of links, and a human clicks them. A neural network reads the content and retells it to the user, often with a link to the source. So content must be not only useful to a human but "easy to read" for a machine.

Why it matters in 2026

  • The user journey is changing. Part of the audience now gets answers straight from AI without visiting sites. If you're not there — you won't be seen.
  • AI assistants became "gatekeepers" They decide which brands to mention and whom to cite. This is a new visibility channel that can't be ignored.
  • The favicon is a face in AI output. Next to a cited link an assistant often shows the site's icon. About it — in the favicon in search and AI answers.

What makes a site "understandable" to AI

Structured data (Schema)

Schema.org markup describes a page's content in a language a machine understands: where the title is, the author, the price, the question‑answer. This helps both search engines and neural networks interpret the content correctly.

FAQ blocks

Clear "question — answer" blocks are an ideal format for AI: assistants easily extract ready answers from them. Plus this structures the article for a human too.

The llms.txt file

llms.txt is a file in the site root, a kind of "content map for neural networks": it tells AI which pages are important and how the site is organized. In spirit it's an analog of robots.txt and a sitemap, but addressed to language models. Separately, brands publish machine‑readable data (for example, a catalog in JSON) that AI can read directly.

Good content for AI = good content for humans

GEO isn't "tricking the algorithm" Neural networks value exactly what a reader does: a clear structure, honest facts, understandable headings, answers to real questions. A clear article with headings, lists, and an FAQ is equally good for a human and an assistant.

How to connect a brand and GEO

For a brand GEO is not only text but asset recognizability:

  • Favicon and logo. The brand icon must be recognizable in an AI answer — see the favicon in search and AI answers.
  • Consistency. The more consistently a brand presents itself, the more reliably AI identifies it — see brand consistency.
  • A light site. A fast, light site is indexed and parsed better — see sustainable web design.
GEO doesn't cancel SEO

Optimizing for AI doesn't replace search optimization but complements it. People still use search, click favicons in results, and visit sites. GEO is another visibility channel, not a replacement for the old ones. Don't drop SEO for GEO — work on both.

The short takeaway

GEO is optimizing a site for neural networks, the analog of SEO for the era of AI search. For an assistant to find, understand, and cite you, a site needs machine‑readable data: Schema markup, a clear structure, FAQ blocks, and an llms.txt file as a content "map" for AI. The role of the favicon and brand consistency grows too — an assistant identifies and shows you by them. The key: good content for AI is the same clear, honest, structured content as for a human, and GEO complements but doesn't replace familiar SEO.
